# The first hour (DESIGN, SPOILERS)

What a player sees, reads and can work out in their first hour through the
door, and what the first hour is for. Written against BIBLE.md, PORTAL.md,
games.md and pinned.md.

**The job of the first hour** is one feeling, not one fact: *this was a
place full of people, and something happened to all of them at once.* The
player should leave the hour knowing that without having read a single
word.

---

## Minute 0: the door

The stone, the phrase, the Portal on the rail, the threshold: the ring, the
phrase, "Solace is behind you." All shipped. Stepping through is the first
thing that isn't.

## Minute 1: your own house, emptied

The Portal opens as a page, and **it is laid out exactly like Solace**:
rail, channel list, message pane, roster. Same shapes, same places. Then
everything is wrong about it:

- Every label is glyphs. The palette is cold greys and bruise violet, dim.
  (It gets brighter as the community reads more of it.)
- **Every room shows unread.** A new Solace account starts caught up;
  here, nothing has been read in fifty-four million years. The badges are
  capped the way Solace's are, so every room says the same thing.
- **The roster is sixty rows, all offline**, each with the same odd mark
  beside the name (ENTRY-X). At the bottom, one new row, **online, with no
  mark: you**, your name in glyphs. Tharvend filed you the moment you
  arrived.

The first chill is structural. A player who has used Solace for months
recognises the shape of a live community, sees it dead, and sees themselves
in it.

## Minutes 2–10: what can be seen without reading

Everything below is visible before a single glyph is unlocked. None of it
is pointed out.

- **Your name is a key.** You know its sounds; the glyphs in your roster row
  are yours to test. So are the names of every other Solace player who has
  come through before you.
- **The last-seen column.** Sixty timestamps, long strings of numerals, and
  every one begins with the same run of digits. You cannot read a number
  yet. You can see they are all nearly the same number.
- **The channel list's last activity.** Some rooms went quiet long before
  others. There is an order to it.
- **The digest's shape.** Scroll *naa-ren* and every post has the same
  skeleton: a line, then blocks, then a sign-off. Scroll further and the
  posts get shorter, block by block, until there is one.
- **The timestamps on the digest** end in a long, identical run of the same
  numeral, with one digit changing post to post. (This is the easiest way
  into the clock, and some players will see it here.)

## Minutes 10–20: trying to speak

- **The composer refuses English.** Type in our letters and the post is not
  filed; Tharvend answers with a status sign, nothing else. The composer
  offers a **glyph keyboard** holding only the glyphs the community has
  unlocked, which at the very start is the handful from players' names and
  the stone.
- **Reactions work.** The six signs are there from the start. Most players'
  first act in the hall is a reaction on a post they cannot read. It is
  filed. It appears beside the reactions of the dead.
- **Nothing answers.** No bot greets you. Tharvend does not speak. The only
  new thing in the hall is what you and the other players do.

## Minutes 20–40: the first game, the first module

The survey is on the hall's rail from the start (games.md). It teaches
itself with no words: rhythms on one side, catalogue entries in glyphs on
the other, and a match lights up. **The Hiccup is the easiest signature in
the catalogue** (a clean tick with a stutter on the eighth), so nearly every
player's first match, and first module, is the last thing Nemaai ever
filed. The module unlocks a glyph for everyone, and the wall in #general
announces who cracked it.

## Minutes 40–55: the voice rooms

Two voice rooms, empty. A player who drops into one alone hears nothing:
room tone, their own breath. Most leave within a minute. The playback fault
(voice.md) is rare enough that nobody meets it in their first hour, and it
must stay that way: the voice rooms should be learned as empty before
anyone finds out they aren't.

## Minute 55: leaving

"Solace is behind you" is always one click away. Leaving writes a line in
Tharvend's log: the player's keystone dropped, **held open**. The next time
they come back, the log shows the line was held for them the whole time
they were gone.

---

## The latecomer's first hour

Decipherment is shared, so a player who arrives months after the first will
find much of the alphabet already cracked, and the first hour changes shape:
they can read sounds, names, the digest's headings. **That must not make it
easy**, and it doesn't, because of what stays personal:

- **The lexicon.** Sounding a word out isn't knowing it. Meanings are
  confirmed one by one, per player.
- **The grammar.** The evidentials, the formulas, the two we's.
- **The notation.** *kaal-ith* is behind a second cipher.
- **The clock.** A latecomer can read the numerals and still not know what a
  tick is; the hydrogen-line crack is theirs to make.

So a latecomer's first hour starts further in and ends in the same place:
able to *see* the story's shape before they can read what it says.

## What the first hour must never do

- Explain anything. No tutorial text, no tooltips in English beyond "Solace
  is behind you."
- Show Makaal, or anything that could be mistaken for it.
- Let Tharvend speak.
- Reward speed. The first hour has one module in it and that is enough.
