# Pinned documents (ARCHIVE DRAFT, SPOILERS)

Solace pins messages; so did Naa'shenai. Each room carries one or two
documents pinned to its head, written long before the window, in the same
script as everything else. They are the hall's reference shelf, and each one
is also a decipherment aid: formal, repetitive, full of words the rooms use
constantly. Written against BIBLE.md.

---

## shenai — THE CHARTER OF NAA'SHENAI

*Pinned by the first keeper. Set down by the fold school of Maalith and the
deep institute of Theruun, eleven long turns and more before the window.*

1. **The hall belongs to no galaxy.** It stands in Nul'ir, where nothing
   lives, so that no people may say it stands on theirs.
2. **The hall speaks one script.** Six galaxies speak six ways. In the hall
   all write the common script, so that no one is a guest in another's
   speech. The keeper will not file what is not written in it.
3. **Every member holds a keystone.** The keystone is the member's way in
   and the hall's way of knowing them. The hall remembers every keystone it
   has issued.
4. **The hall keeps unassigned keystones** for members not yet admitted.
   They are seats, and a seat is a promise.
5. **What is said in the hall is kept.** A member may withdraw what they
   said. The hall keeps that it was said.
6. **The keeper keeps the hall and the record of it.** It takes no side.
   It holds no opinion. Do not ask it for one; it has none to give, and it
   will only file the question.
7. **The door beneath listens.** When a keystone speaks to the hall, the
   hall asks: *the door beneath listens still.* The keystone answers: *I
   answer from the far shore.* Then it is let in.
8. **Nothing in the hall is secret except where it is.** The keeper knows
   where. The keeper will not say.

Signed: for the fold school, **Anvaar Ilaar**. For the deep institute,
**Tethi Dar**. For the hall, the keeper, with the count mark.

> **Notes.** The signatories carry two cast surnames: Senneth's and
> Aveth's lines founded the hall three hundred years before their
> descendants signed and objected to the long hold. Nobody in the archive
> remarks on it. Article 2 is the in-world reason players cannot post in
> English. Article 7 is where the shipped phrase comes from, and a player
> who deciphers the charter mid-game realises they have been saying both
> halves of a handshake. Article 8 is why the Eshai themselves never knew
> where their hall was.

## shenai — ISHAAL, THE RING GAME (pinned by Nemaai)

*"The rules, since Paelu asked. Tamruk will say I've left something out. I
haven't."*

- **The board** is eight rings and eight spokes: sixty-four points where
  they cross. The outermost ring is **the edge**. The innermost is **the
  long ring**, because it is a long way to it.
- **Each player has eight stones.** At the start, one player's stones sit on
  the two outer rings at the even spokes, the other's on the same rings at
  the odd spokes.
- **A move** is one stone, one step: along its ring (either way; the rings
  are round) or along its spoke (in or out), to an empty point.
- **Taking.** A stone is taken when an opponent's stones sit on both sides
  of it along its ring, or on both sides of it along its spoke. You may move
  into a point between two enemy stones without being taken; you are only
  taken by a move.
- **Holding the long ring.** You win when, at the start of your move, four
  of your stones are on the long ring. You also win if your opponent is down
  to two stones.
- **By relay, the custom is one move each per turn.** Games last years. A
  player who misses a move without saying why has conceded, which is why
  nobody ever misses one.

*"The fifth stone is always bait. Everyone knows. Everyone takes it anyway."*

> **Notes.** This is game 2 in PORTAL.md, and these rules are complete
> enough to build. The Nemaai–Tamruk game stands at move 757 when the room
> goes dark, with Paelu to move. **The build needs the full move list**: a
> plausible game of 757 moves, generated to fit every move the archive
> names (612 at t0; 640/641 at t11; 660 at t19; the bait; 703; 750 early;
> Tamruk's 752, fifth stone to the long ring, played by Nemaai at t72; 753
> to 757). A game position is not archive text, so generating it is allowed.
> A player who learns the rules can open the board and see whose move it is.

## naa-ren — HESH ANVAAR'S RULES FOR THE DIGEST

*Pinned by the third curator. Oruun has never unpinned it.*

1. Print what was heard. What was heard can be checked.
2. Do not print what was told. What was told can only be checked against
   the teller, and the teller is in another galaxy.
3. Read the cluster from the deep edge inward, in the order of the relay.
   Always the same order, so that a reader can find their own galaxy without
   looking.
4. One heading for each galaxy, every turn, even if it says only
   *nothing.*
5. If nothing is heard from a galaxy, print its heading and say so. A
   heading left out is a galaxy left out.
6. The hall's line last. The hall is where the digest is read, not where it
   is made.
7. Sign it. A digest nobody signed is a rumour.

> **Notes.** Oruun breaks rule 2 at t24 and apologises. She breaks rule 5
> from t21 on, because she cannot bring herself to print a heading over
> nothing forever, and instead prints the count of headings at the top. A
> player who deciphers these rules early reads every digest after that as a
> list of rules being broken one at a time.

## Oshkaa — HOW AN ENTRY IS MADE (the Great Index)

*The form every entry in the count follows. Pinned for the new
indexers, who keep asking.*

1. **The name**, and every name they were called.
2. **The line**: whose they were, and who was theirs.
3. **What they kept**: a reading, a garden, a ship, a room, a child, a
   problem. Everyone kept something.
4. **One true thing**, said by someone who knew them. It does not have to
   be important. It has to be true.
5. **The count mark.**

*"Nothing is lost that someone kept." — the Index's motto, over every
reading room door*

> **Notes.** This is where "one true thing" comes from: the Assembly asks
> for one at the star-naming (Maalith room, t2.1), and the last room is
> built on it. The count mark drawn here is the same glyph as the last
> room's name: the one place it appears besides the room itself. **This
> breaks the "appears exactly once" rule in PORTAL.md**, so the pinned form
> must show the mark as an EMPTY BOX, a space for it, with the instruction
> "draw the mark here." The glyph itself stays unique to the last room.

## Senwe — THE SURVEY OATH

*Said by every survey-keeper at their first posting. Pinned by the
council.*

We look, and we do not touch.
If we are standing on it, it is not what it was.
We count what lives, so that it is counted.
We leave it as we found it, and we write down that we did.

> **Notes.** Olun Theth said this at the start of the window and was sent
> to Theruun, where there was nothing to count.

## Paaruk — THE FLEET'S STANDING ORDERS (excerpt)

*Pinned by the yards, "so the families know what they're for."*

1. The Fleet moves what would otherwise fall.
2. The Fleet goes where the Assembly sends it and does what the
   circumstance requires. The Assembly will not always know what that is.
   The Fleet will.
3. An unattended instrument found running is to be released at its own
   controls. Nothing is to be left running that nobody is watching.
4. A hull without its crew is to be brought home.
5. The Fleet does not fight. There has never been anything to fight.

*Beneath, in a different hand:* Hold her steady. Everything leans
eventually. — T.H.

> **Notes.** Order 3 is why the Fleet released the deep array at Theruun
> (digest t29). Order 4 is the one nobody could follow at t40. Order 5 is
> the one the archive takes apart.

## Maalith — THE PULSE NIGHT PROGRAMME (ren-ta'an)

*Nemaai's running list, pinned in the voice room's description and updated
as stars are added.*

The near stars, in the order they come up over Maalith. Sixty-four of them,
each by its own beat. **One pause** in the middle, the length of a pulse,
for the one that stopped a long time ago and is kept in the programme
anyway. **Added this turn:** the Hiccup, out past Yulenai. It stutters on
every eighth tick. Play it twice for Sethe.

> **Notes.** The programme is never updated after t19.9. Nemaai's final
> sound-map (t76.5) is a separate file in the hall; the pinned programme
> still lists all sixty-four near stars and the Hiccup, which is correct:
> Maalith's own sky is still there at the end.

## kaal-ith — THE FOLD SCHOOL'S NOTATION PRIMER (page one)

*Pinned for students. Page one of eight; the other seven were in the fold
school's library in Maalith.*

Page one covers numbers, units and the particles: enough to read a
measurement, never enough to read an argument. It is a decipher module on
its own: the first crack in the second cipher. Its text (written 2026-10-30):

**Page one. Numbers, units, and how a number knows.**

- **Numbers** are written in the common numerals. A point separates the
  whole from its parts, eighths first.
- **The tick** is one beat of cold hydrogen. Every bench counts in ticks.
- **The breath** is how long a fold is held, for teaching. A paper never
  says breath; it says ticks.
- **The turn** is the digest's turn. **The long turn** is a generation.
- **Depth** is how far a fold reaches, counted in steps from the bench.
- **The load** is what a coupling carries. Every coupling has a design load;
  write the reading beside it.
- **The far-end term** is what the far end of a fold gives back. Write it
  every time, even when it is nothing. Especially then.
- **How a number knows.** Mark every number: *-esh*, measured on this bench;
  *-tol*, calculated; *-ren*, received from another bench. Never *-vaa*. A
  number that was told is not a number.

*Pages two to eight are in the library.*

> **Notes.** The rest of the notation must be deduced from kaal-ith itself,
> which is the act-two puzzle. Senneth's final post (t79.9) is written so
> that page one plus the room is exactly enough to read it, which a careful
> player may notice was deliberate.
