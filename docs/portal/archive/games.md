# The Portal's games (DESIGN, SPOILERS)

Three games, all things the Eshai themselves did, never games of chance (they
had none). Each one is a decipher-module source and each one teaches a skill
the archive needs. Written against BIBLE.md; the story beats they touch are
marked. Nothing here pays silver or anything that crosses back into Solace
(PORTAL.md → the faucet doctrine).

---

## 1. The survey (build first)

**What it is in the fiction.** The Yulenai outer survey mirrored its
readings to the hall for three hundred years, so that the whole cluster
could look over its shoulder. The mirror is still there: a sky instrument
in the hall with a timeline you can scrub.

**How it plays.**
- A sky map of Ilumaar: six galaxies, each resolved into its pulsars, each
  pulsar a beat. The player tunes the instrument (frequency, gain, a
  window of time) and matches pulse signatures against the survey's
  catalogue, which is written in glyphs and numerals.
- **Matching is the teaching.** A signature is a rhythm; its catalogue entry
  is a glyph-string and an octal period. Matching rhythms to entries is
  frequency analysis wearing a different coat, and it trains the eye for
  the script. Each fully matched galaxy yields a module.
- **The Hiccup** is in the catalogue, added at t19.9, the last entry
  Nemaai ever filed there. It stutters every eighth tick. It is also one of
  the easiest signatures in the game, so most players meet it first.
- **The one that stopped a long time ago** is in the catalogue too, marked
  with a pause. It was dead before the window. It is the only dead source a
  player meets in the first hours, and it teaches what a silence looks like
  on the instrument.

**The story it carries.** Scrub the timeline forward through the window and
the six galaxies do not change: light takes far longer than four years to
cross between them, so the survey sees the cluster "as it was" to the end,
exactly as Esko kept saying. **What the survey does record is the relay's
fold weather**, the channel-thinning Senneth measured, as a faint band the
instrument can overlay. Turn the overlay on and scrub, and a player watches
the thinning run down the chain, channel by channel, in the digest's order,
before any text says so. They can re-derive the finding of t44 themselves.
PORTAL.md asked for exactly that: "worth more than any message saying so."

**The Near Dark.** The survey can be pointed past the edge of the cluster,
as Esko's survey always was. It shows nothing. Every sweep, every window,
every gain. It is the only place in the game where the instrument is never
wrong and never interesting, and players will point it there anyway,
because they will want the survey to be wrong. (Hanu, last room, t80.173.)

## 2. Ishaal, the ring game

**What it is in the fiction.** The hall's own board game, played by relay
one move per turn. Rules are pinned in shenai (pinned.md), complete enough
to build.

**How it plays.**
- **Against other players**, live, at any pace: the Portal's only social
  game. A match over a turn-long clock is the Eshai way; a quick board is
  allowed.
- **Against the hall's recorded games.** Tharvend kept every game ever
  played in the hall. A player can replay them, and learn the game from
  people who played it for years. The Nemaai–Tamruk game is among them, 757
  moves, and for most of the dig it is just a very long game.
- **The rules must be read to be learned.** The game is available from the
  start, but its move hints are in glyphs; learning the pinned rules is the
  first complete document most players read.
- Winning against a recorded opponent's opening, or finishing a replay
  without error, yields modules.

**The unfinished game.** The board stands at move 757, Paelu to move, the
room dark. A player may make Paelu's move. **Proposal: Tharvend files it**
(`FILED · ishaal · move 760 [octal], keystone <n>`) and the board then waits
for Nemaai's reply, which never comes, forever, like every other line in
the log. Nothing else happens. It should be the only game in the Portal
that cannot be finished.

## 3. Senneth's bench (act three)

**What it is in the fiction.** The fold school's teaching bench, mirrored
to kaal-ith so students anywhere could run the standard exercises. It can
simulate a held fold.

**How it plays.**
- The player sets a depth and holds a simulated fold, balancing the
  couplings the way Kiru did, while the bench logs the numbers kaal-ith is
  full of: coupling load, release signature, **the far-end term**.
- The exercises are the ones in the archive, in order: depth 14 (the paper),
  depth 15 (with the transient), depth 16, the long hold. Reproducing each
  published result, to the notation's precision, yields a module and unlocks
  the next.
- **The long hold is the last exercise.** The bench runs it for as long as
  the player will watch. The far-end term sits at zero. Then it drifts, in
  the last place, within tolerance, exactly as Kiru logged at t17.9. The
  bench does nothing else. It just keeps holding.
- **The last bench exercise is the unaimed fold**: Paelu's wrong answer,
  Senneth's working from t79.9. The bench opens it, holds it, closes it, and
  reports the far-end term as *unknown*, which is the point (Thovi, t78.7).

**The rule.** The bench never tells the player what the drift means. There
is no alarm, no cutscene, no text. A player who has read kaal-ith knows what
number they are watching. A player who hasn't sees a number move.

## Where the modules come from

Thirty-four glyphs (26 letters, 8 numerals), the notation primer's page
one, and the last room's count mark. Spread across the house on purpose, so
the Portal pulls people back through Solace.

| Source | Modules | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Your own name** | 0, but several free glyphs | Tharvend transliterates it on the roster at arrival. The first crib. |
| **The stone** | 1 | *onnith* is cut into the Stone of the Abyss's art. The first glyph anyone owns. |
| **Sterling** | 3 | All the Foundry ever managed from its stone in three hundred years of trying. He is ashamed of the number. |
| **Backrooms relics** | 6 | Etched relics, one module each, spread across levels (the Rusted Key's pillar). |
| **The survey** | 6 | One per galaxy matched. |
| **Ishaal** | 4 | Openings learned, replays finished. |
| **Senneth's bench** | 4 | Depth 14, 15, 16, the long hold. The unaimed fold exercise is the notation primer's reward instead. |
| **Deduction** | the rest | The claim table: frequency analysis, few enough attempts that guessing is worthless. Where the actual fun lives. |
| **The notation primer, page one** | 1 | Found pinned in kaal-ith. Unlocks the second cipher's first page. |
| **The count mark** | 1, last | Given, never cracked: it appears once in the whole archive. Opens the last room. |

Decipherment is SHARED (PORTAL.md): one community wall, individual credit.
Modules unlock glyphs for everyone; the lexicon, the grammar, the notation
and the clock stay personal work.
