## How this room works

- **Cadence: one digest every turn.** A turn (*keth*) is 8¹⁷ ticks, about
  18.35 of our days. Digests are posted on the turn exactly, so **every digest
  timestamp ends in seventeen zeros and only one digit changes from one to the
  next.** This is the easiest way into the clock. A player who notices the
  column of identical endings has found the turn, and the rest of the calendar
  follows from it.
- **Numbers in this file are octal**, as they appear on screen. Digest numbers
  count from the digest's founding: 13741 (octal) is the 6,113th digest, about
  three hundred of our years of it. Oruun is its fourth curator.
- **The archive starts mid-stream.** The hall kept a rolling window of the
  recent count; everything older went into the deep count on Maalith, and
  Maalith is gone. The window holds about 80 digests, from 13741 to the last.
- **The shape never changes, which is the point.** An opening line with the
  number of headings and the state of the relays; one heading per galaxy in a
  fixed order; a line for the hall; her sign-off. When a galaxy goes dark its
  heading stops appearing, and her opening count drops: *six headings*, then
  *five*. The shape is how a player sees the end coming before they can read
  it.
- **Oruun's rule on evidentials.** She prints what was **heard over relay**
  (*-ren*) and what she **saw herself** in Maalith (*-esh*). She does not print
  what somebody was told (*-vaa*). She says so, once, in 13745. **In movement 2
  the only thing anybody knows about Theruun is secondhand, and she prints it
  anyway, marked *-vaa*, with an apology.** Breaking her own rule is her whole
  arc in one particle.
- Evidentials are shown below as a tag at the end of each item: `[ren]` heard
  over relay · `[esh]` seen · `[tol]` it follows · `[vaa]` told.
- Fixed order of headings: **THERUUN · OSHKAA · PAARUK · SENWE · YULENAI ·
  MAALITH**, then **THE HALL**. Theruun is first because the digest has always
  read the cluster from the deep edge inward.

## The threads movement 1 plants

Each thread is carried by one heading and is quiet enough to read as ordinary
news the first time through.

- **THERUUN, the deep array.** The deepest fold experiments in the cluster,
  reaching record depths. One pause for "a transient, instrument not sky." The
  archive never connects the array to anything. A reader can.
- **OSHKAA, the Great Index.** The Oshkaa indexers keep the count: every name
  ever entered, every record of every life. It tells players what the Eshai
  believed (see below), and so what Makaal takes from them.
- **PAARUK, the yards and the fleet.** Star-lifting, hull-building, and the
  Ninefold Fleet doing what it has always done: moving planets out of the way
  of stars, catching rogue bodies. It has never fought anything. This is why
  "the fleet will handle it" is a reasonable thing to believe.
- **SENWE, the life surveys.** Cataloguing life on other worlds, forbidden to
  touch it. The Eshai revere life, and that reverence is what makes Makaal
  obscene to them.
- **YULENAI, the frontier.** Young settlements, first-look ceremonies, and the
  outer survey that watches the dark beyond the cluster. Paelu comes from here.
- **MAALITH, home.** The relay, the academies, the Assembly of the Count,
  Nemaai's star-maps. Oruun lives here, so this is where her *-esh* items come
  from.
- **THE HALL.** One line about Naa'shenai itself, from Tharvend's status. It is
  almost always the same line.

## What they believed: the count

The Eshai have no afterlife. They have **the count** (*vend*): the record of
every life, kept whole. When one of them dies they are **entered into the
count**, and a life that is kept is a life that is not over. The Oshkaa
indexers are the nearest thing they have to priests, and a death notice in the
digest is a line saying somebody was entered.

**This is what makes Makaal the worst thing that could happen to this people
in particular.** It does not only kill. It erases every trace of a life, and
the count needs a trace to keep. The dead of movement 3 onward are not entered
into anything; there is nothing to enter. And the only record of any of them
that survived is the one Tharvend, "keeper of the count," still keeps.
