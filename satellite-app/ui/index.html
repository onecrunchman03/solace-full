How hosting works

This app runs your community on this PC. Messages and files need no setup at all — the app connects out to the Solace server and everything flows through that connection.

Only voice calls use your internet connection directly, and that needs two port-forwarding rules on your router. Without them, calls still work — they just run on the main server instead of your PC.

Port forwarding for voice

Add these two rules in your router's settings, both pointing at this PC (…):

TypePort(s)What it's for
TCP7880call setup (the Solace server connects here)
UDP50000–50060the voice and video itself
TCP7881backup path for viewers whose networks block UDP

How, on most routers:

  1. Open your router's admin page — usually 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1 in a browser (the address and password are often printed on the router itself).
  2. Find Port Forwarding (sometimes under Advanced, NAT, or Virtual Server).
  3. Add the two rules above, with this PC's address as the target.
  4. Give this PC a reserved/static address in the router's DHCP settings if you can — otherwise the rules can stop working after a reboot when the PC gets a new address.

The first time voice starts, Windows Firewall will ask to allow livekit-server — allow it on private networks. The Status tab's voice line tells you whether hosting is live.

Can this PC handle the bandwidth?

When voice runs on this PC, every stream costs your upload its bitrate once per viewer. Rough costs per person watching:

WhatUpload per viewer
Voice only~0.05 Mbps
Camera~1.5 Mbps
Screen share 1080p~5–8 Mbps
Screen share 4K~16 Mbps

So one 1080p screen share with 4 viewers wants ~25 Mbps of upload. Check your plan's upload speed (it's usually much lower than download).

Three protections are automatic: viewers on weak connections get served a lower-quality copy instead of stalling everyone, quality adapts to what your connection can actually push, and if this PC is unreachable entirely, calls quietly run on the main Solace server instead. Watch the Diagnostics tab during a busy call — "call traffic" and the network line show the real load, and a warning appears if this PC starts dropping packets.

Good to know

  • Closing the window hides it to the tray; the community stays online. Quit from the tray icon to take it offline. Turn on Launch when Windows starts in Settings and it survives reboots.
  • Backups: pick a folder in Settings → Automatic daily backup — a second drive or a NAS share is ideal — and the app keeps a daily snapshot there (last 7 days of messages, all files, and the community's identity) by itself. Storage can move too: Settings → Community storage → Move puts the messages and files on any drive you choose.
  • Updates are automatic. The app checks the server and offers "Install & restart" when a new version is ready.
  • New PC or lost machine? Ask a Solace admin for a rotation key — paste it into a fresh install and your community comes back under the same page (restore your backup first to keep the history).
  • "Reconnecting…" means the Solace server can't be reached — it retries by itself. "Delisted" means the server has switched your satellite off; talk to an admin.
  • You manage the community itself — channels, joining rules, members — inside Solace, on your page, like any manager.
