Privacy Policy

Version 2 · Effective October 12, 2026

The short version. Solace doesn't want your email, doesn't run trackers or ads, and doesn't sell anything about you. It keeps what it needs to run a chat service and keep accounts safe: your account details, what you post, and the address you sign in from. Voice and video aren't recorded. Communities hosted on a member's computer keep their messages on that member's computer.

Who is responsible

Solace is run by an individual operator (the "Operator") in the United States. The Operator is responsible for the data described here, except for content stored by satellite hosts, which is described below.

What we collect

Things you give us

  • Account: a username, a display name, a password (stored only as a salted hash), and a recovery secret (also stored only as a hash — we can't read it back). We do not ask for an email address, phone number, real name, or payment details.
  • Profile: anything you choose to add — avatar, banner, bio, epitaph, cosmetics, status, preferences such as theme and keybinds.
  • Content: messages in channels, direct messages, reactions, images and files you upload, custom sounds, and everything you do in the games (spins, bets, trades, loans, achievements).
  • Reports and feedback you file, and messages you send the admin.

Things we record automatically

  • Sign-in security: the IP address you connect from, the network it belongs to, your browser's user-agent string, and the time and outcome of each sign-in attempt. This is how Solace notices a sign-in from a new place and asks for your recovery secret, and how the Operator spots someone trying to break into accounts.
  • Sessions: a random token stored in your browser or app so you stay signed in, with the address and device it was issued to.
  • Server logs and error reports from the app, which can include your user id and what you were doing when something broke.
  • Push notifications: if you turn them on, a subscription your browser generates, so Solace can wake your device for a DM or a mention. Turn them off and it's deleted.
  • Your agreement to these documents: which version you accepted, when, and the address and browser it came from. If you host a community, the same for the Host Agreement, plus the network your satellite connects from.

Things we don't collect

No analytics, no advertising identifiers, no third-party trackers, no fingerprinting, no location beyond what an IP address implies, and no recording of voice, video, or screen shares.

How we use it

  • To run Solace — deliver messages, run the games, keep your preferences.
  • To keep accounts safe — the sign-in ledger, the new-network challenge, lockouts, session revocation.
  • To moderate — reviewing reports, enforcing the Guidelines, and keeping records of moderation actions.
  • To comply with the law — preserving and disclosing content when a valid legal process requires it, and reporting child sexual abuse material as the law requires.
  • To fix things — server logs and error reports.
  • To let the house characters answer you — when you message Sterling, Dr. Voss or the Goblin Lenders, or mention them in a channel, your message, the recent conversation, and the facts about your own account they need (such as your silver or what you owe) are read by a language model running on a computer the Operator owns to write the reply. Nothing is sent to an outside AI company. The model itself keeps nothing, but each of the three keeps a few short notes about you from your private conversations with them, so they remember you next time. Those notes are only ever used in your own private conversations with that character, you can read them and erase them from that conversation ("What … remembers"), and they're deleted with your account. They may also recall messages from public channels, which everyone can already read. Each of the three also keeps a standing with you — a score from your activity with them (talking, trading, playing) that changes how they speak to you — shown in your conversation with them and deleted with your account.

Who can see what

  • Other members see your public profile, your messages in rooms they can read, your reactions, your presence (online, in voice, in a game), your collection, and your standing with the goblins if you owe them. Direct messages are visible only to the two people in them and to the Operator when a report or legal obligation requires a look.
  • Moderators can act on accounts (bans, suspensions, invites) and manage the pages they run. They don't see reports, and they can't see your password, recovery secret, or sign-in addresses.
  • The Operator can see everything stored on the server, including direct messages, and uses that access only for the purposes above.
  • Satellite hosts. When you post in a community hosted on a member's own computer, that message and any files are stored on their machine. The host can read everything in their community and is responsible for keeping it safe, under the Host Agreement. The Operator's server keeps an index (who posted, when, which channel), reactions, and details of attached files — and holds an uploaded file briefly while it moves to the host's machine — and passes the content through. When you search, your search words are also sent to the hosts of the communities you belong to, so their messages can be found; a host you don't belong to never receives them.
  • Service providers. Push notifications pass through your browser vendor's push service (Apple, Google, or Mozilla) as an encrypted payload. Voice and video flow through a media server the Operator runs, or through a satellite host's own media server when they host calls. No other third party receives your data.
  • Authorities. We disclose data when a valid subpoena, court order, or other legal process requires it, or when we believe in good faith it's necessary to prevent serious harm. Child sexual abuse material is reported to NCMEC as required by US law.

We do not sell your data and we don't share it for advertising.

How long we keep it

  • Messages stay until they're deleted. Deleting one removes its text and its files.
  • Uploaded files in chat expire on their own — animated images after about 30 days, other images and files after about 90 days. The message stays with an "expired" tile. Avatars, banners and sounds stay until you replace or remove them.
  • Sign-in records are kept for 90 days.
  • Server logs rotate within weeks. Error reports and the moderation audit trail are kept until the Operator clears them; error reports from hosted communities are kept for up to a year.
  • Backups of the database and uploads are taken nightly and rotated; a deleted item can persist in a backup for a limited time after it's gone from the live service.
  • A deleted account is anonymized immediately: profile, uploads, preferences, and virtual belongings are removed, and messages you posted in shared rooms remain attributed to "deleted user" so conversations still read correctly. The record that an account existed and when it signed in stays in the security ledger for the retention period above.

Your choices

  • Edit or remove your profile details at any time.
  • Delete your own messages.
  • Block members so they can't message or trade with you.
  • Turn notifications off per device.
  • See your active sessions and recent sign-ins, and sign out other devices, in Settings → Security.
  • Delete your account in Settings → Security. It's immediate and can't be undone.
  • Ask the Operator for a copy of the data held about you, or to correct it, by messaging the admin or emailing [LEGAL EMAIL].

Age

Solace is for adults. We don't knowingly keep data about anyone under 18; if we learn an account belongs to a minor we close it and remove its data.

Security

Everything travels over HTTPS. Passwords and recovery secrets are hashed with a slow, salted algorithm. Sign-ins from a new network need your recovery secret. Sessions can be revoked. The server is hardened and updated, and its security state is reviewed regularly. No system is perfect; if there's ever a breach affecting you, we'll tell you.

Where the data lives

On a server in the United States operated by the Operator. If you use Solace from elsewhere, your data is transferred to and handled in the US.

Changes

When this policy changes materially, the version number goes up and the app asks you to accept the new version.
