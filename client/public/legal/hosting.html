Satellite Host Agreement

Version 1 · Effective September 14, 2026 · Adds to the Terms of Service

The short version. A satellite is a community you run on your own computer. Its messages live on your machine, and it's yours: you're responsible for what's in it and for keeping it clean, the same way the operator is responsible for the main server. The operator can disconnect it at any time. You accept this agreement when you redeem a provisioning key.

1. What a satellite is

Solace lets a member run a community ("Satellite") on their own computer using the Solace Satellite app or the Ubuntu installer. The Satellite's messages, files, and history are stored only on your machine. The Solace server (the "Hub", run by the "Operator") keeps an index of who posted what and when (plus reactions and file details, and a file briefly while it moves to your machine), relays your community's content to its members, and lets them find it as a page in Solace. It also records your acceptance of this agreement and the network your Satellite connects from. You, the person who set the Satellite up, are its "Host" — an independent member, not the Operator's agent, employee or partner.

2. Accepting this agreement

You accept this agreement when you redeem a provisioning key — by ticking the box in the Satellite app, answering yes in the Ubuntu installer, or passing the acceptance flag to the command line. The Hub records that acceptance. You must be 18 or older and a member of Solace in good standing to host.

3. You are the operator of your community

  • Your content, your responsibility. You are solely responsible for everything stored on your Satellite and for everything your members post there. The Operator does not host it, does not pre-screen it, and is not responsible for it.
  • The rules apply. The Community Guidelines apply inside your community exactly as they do on the Hub. You must enforce them: remove content that breaks them, remove members who post it (and switch joining to request or invite so they can't walk back in), and tell the Operator about repeat offenders, who can be removed from Solace altogether.
  • The law applies. You must comply with the laws that apply to you and to your community, including copyright law and the laws about reporting child sexual abuse material. If you find content that the law requires to be reported, report it to the authorities and tell the Operator immediately.
  • Your members are Solace members. Everyone in your community is bound by the Solace Terms. You may not use your community to get around a Hub decision (a ban, a removal) about a member or their content.

4. Working with the Operator

  • Respond to a request from the Operator about content in your community — a takedown, a question, a request to preserve something — within 48 hours.
  • Keep the Satellite software up to date. The app updates itself; the Ubuntu install can be updated with one command. Running a modified daemon, or one altered to evade Hub controls, is not allowed.
  • Keep your machine reasonably secure: patched, with your identity file and data protected. Your Satellite's private key is the credential for your community; if it's stolen, tell the Operator so the key can be rotated.
  • Don't collect, export, or use your members' data for anything except running the community.

5. What the Operator may do

  • Disconnect ("delist") your Satellite at any time, with or without notice, for a breach of this agreement, the Guidelines, or the law, on a report, or for any other reason the Operator considers necessary. Delisting removes your community from Solace; your data stays on your machine.
  • Remove or hide content the Hub relays, and act on your members' accounts under the Terms.
  • Stop relaying, change the hosting features, or end satellite hosting altogether. Hosting is provided free, "as is", with no promise that it will keep working, that calls will connect, or that your community will stay reachable.
  • Preserve and disclose the index the Hub holds, and anything relayed through it, when the law requires.

6. Data

You hold your members' content. Keep it safe, keep it only as long as you need it, and delete it if a member asks you to delete their own contributions or the Operator asks you to remove something. Back it up if you care about it; if your machine dies, your community's history dies with it, and the Operator can't restore it. If you shut your Satellite down, tell your members first.

7. Copyright

If a copyright holder complains about content in your community, remove it promptly. The Operator will forward any notice received about your community to you, and may remove the content from the relay or delist the community if you don't act. Repeat infringement by your members must end with their removal from your community.

8. Things you must not host

Anything the Guidelines forbid; anything illegal where you or your members are; a community whose purpose is to harass, defraud, or harm anyone; or a community used to distribute malware, stolen data, or pirated material.
